1 · What was measured

For every catalogue pixel of existing_faults.tif (60894 cells inside the 5165840-cell footprint) we walk a line perpendicular to the local trace strike, ±400 m at 25 m sampling, and locate the nearest crest of the DEM-curvature scarp (det_elev, the maximum of -d²z/dn²) and the nearest ridge of the magnetic gradient (|tmi_hg|), with |iso_grav_anom_hg| kept as an independent corroboration channel. Strike comes from a 5×5 structure tensor of the catalogue itself; the eigenvector sign is canonicalised so that a trace cannot place dots on both sides. A crest is believed only if it is an interior local maximum with full smoothing support, clears 25% of the strongest convex break in its own transect, and — for the gated numbers — exceeds the 90th percentile of crest heights measured at points that have no fault anywhere (17.57 curvature units DEM, 94.10 magnetic).

Coverage: 60756 pixels have a usable strike, 59805 transects are complete, a DEM crest was found on 44390 and a magnetic ridge on 40063; both on 29557 (48.5% of the catalogue). The correlation between the DEM offset and the magnetic offset is r = 0.022 — the two datasets do not agree on any displacement, which is the single most informative number on this page.

The estimator returns a median |offset| of 1.323 px at points chosen ≥5 px away from any fault, and 1.394 px on catalogue pixels sampled in a random direction instead of across the strike. Any claim of a displacement smaller than about half a pixel is inside that floor: the raw, uncalibrated catalogue median of 1.476 px is the noise of the instrument, not a property of the faults. (Logging this is IR-56-007 — the first pass of this experiment looked like a small positive result and was not one.)

The detector's own accuracy, measured on synthetic scarps with a closed-form crest position (evidence/estimator_validation.json): detection is complete for |offset| ≤ 2 px, the scatter is 0.0 px, and there is a constant placement bias of 0.329 px (max 0.462 px) that depends on scarp width, not on the offset. So a real 2 px displacement would measure 1.5–2.5 px — it would not measure 0.0, which is what we see — but no absolute sub-pixel claim is made anywhere in this repository.

2 · Corridors: is any trace displaced?

A single pixel agreeing with itself is not a displaced map line. We group catalogue pixels into 8-connected components and require, per component: ≥12 usable pixels, ≥75% putting the displacement on the same side in both families, ≥60% of pixels corroborated within 1 px, a median joint offset ≥ 2 px, and a robust z ≥ 3 with the standard error floored at the LiDAR-measured precision of the estimator (1.296 px — without the floor a four-pixel corridor with a collapsed MAD reached z = 60.6, IR-56-008).

That last row is a limit on the instrument, stated rather than buried: the RMS disagreement between the coarse and fine measurements (180.9 m) is larger than a competition cell, so a per-pixel coarse-grid offset carries almost no information about where the 3 m crest is. This lane may therefore speak about distributions, correlations and corridor medians — never about the correction owed to one individual pixel. That is why the emission rule demands a corridor.

5 · Control on the cluster rule — and what it changes

The emission rule also requires 3 of 9 neighbouring candidate pixels, which is our operational reading of "consistent". That is only evidence if a coherent offset produces more neighbour-agreement than an incoherent one, so we tested it against two nulls built from the same 5612 candidate pixels:

This is the one place where the corrected instrument changed the science, and it is not a win for the hypothesis. The sub-2 px agreement between the DEM crest and the magnetic ridge is genuinely coherent along the traces: 680 surviving pixels where reassigning the same offsets yields 153. Something real is displacing the geomorphic lineation from the catalogue by about a pixel in places. What kills the emission is scale and independence: the coherence lives at 0.77 px (median joint offset), which is below the 2 px bar the brief sets and below the estimator's own noise floor of 1.32 px, and at 3 m resolution not one of 13 segments reaches 200 m. A coherent 1 px wobble is not a correctable registration error, and the holdout cannot even see one (arm D vs B). So we report the coherence as a finding about the evidence and the catalogue, and we still emit nothing that we would defend.

6 · Uniqueness against every earlier raster

Shared lane gate on the final dots: duplicate = True, ok = False  485 priors, 0 read errors

The template's own gate (gates.lane_uniqueness_report, phase dots, full exact rank over 5165840 eligible rank cells, 270 distinct decoded priors) reports max Spearman ρ = 0.0051 against a 0.90 threshold (pass), and directed ≤3 px dot proximity = 1.000 against a 0.70 threshold (fail, 105 offending priors). The rank test passes by a mile; the proximity test fails for the arithmetic reason given below, and the protocol says a proximity trigger is logged as a duplicate and the lane stops. Both of our rasters are therefore not eligible for a submission slot by this run's own gate, and we are not arguing our way past it: we are reporting the gate's verdict and the reason it fires, so a selector can decide with the numbers visible.

We enumerated the git trees of the sibling repositories and downloaded every single-band, grid-aligned .tif between 80 KB and 2.6 MB under their submission/registry paths: 485 rasters, 0 read errors, scored in one pass by exact directed ≤3 px dot proximity in both directions (evidence/registry_screen_v1_rows.jsonl). That is the dot-level view; the earlier session in this repo built the hash-level view (docs/research/registry-index.json: 944 sibling TIFs, 920 on this grid, 432 sharing a hash with another file). The two are complementary — a hash match would catch a literal copy, a proximity match catches a re-derivation — and neither finds a duplicate of these rasters in the reciprocal direction.
